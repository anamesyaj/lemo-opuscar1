## 1. Three candidate structures (DIRECTOR.md §4)

**A. One night, one sleeper.** Follow a single sleeper through one painted night; sleep cycles render as waves of paint over the bed; the alarm is the antagonist. Intimate, character-led.
**B. The paradox, then the mechanism.** Open on the hook (8 hours, still exhausted) → reveal sleep architecture (90-minute cycles) → the three thieves that shatter cycles → the one fix. Classic explainer spine.
**C. Myth-bust countdown.** "Three reasons eight hours isn't enough" — listicle energy, punchy.

**Chosen: B.** It carries the research hook verbatim ("You slept 8 hours last night. So why do you feel like you got hit by a truck?"), gives the impasto medium its best material (cycles as painted waves, grey→colour re-laid as understanding dawns), and fits 60 seconds without rushing. A is lovely but needs a character the series doesn't have yet; C is better saved for the #20 tax-brackets myth-bust.

**Opening image:** a dark bedroom in near-monochrome impasto; one red alarm clock glowing — the one coloured thing.
**Ending:** morning light rakes across the same bedroom, now in full colour; the sleeper rises clear-eyed. Echoes the opening.
**Score shape:** low ambient bed → gentle piano motif entering with the mechanism → warm swell at the fix → resolve.

## 2. Logline and arc

**Logline:** A 60-second painted explainer revealing why eight hours of sleep can still leave you exhausted — and the single fix that matters most.

**Arc:** Setup (the paradox: 8 hours, still tired) → Turn (sleep is architecture, not a bank — 90-minute cycles) → Complication (the three thieves shatter the cycles) → Ending (the fix: protect the rhythm; colour returns).

## 3. Benchmark

**Learn from:**
- *The Umbrellas of Cherbourg* (1964) opening titles — overhead choreography read as moving colour, music-locked editing. Learn: one action = one beat; colour as event.
- Afremov-style palette-knife street painting — knife mosaic, vertical wet reflections, warm lights vs cool shadow. Learn: stroke language, temperature structure (cool night → warm morning).

**Don't take:** their compositions, characters, melodies, or any specific shot. Our film is its own bedroom, its own sleeper, its own score.

## 5. Beat sheet (second by second)

- **0–3** — Night bedroom, monochrome, red alarm clock. Narration hook.
- **3–10** — "The answer isn't hours — it's architecture." Sleep waves roll under the sleeper.
- **10–20** — 90-minute cycles painted: light → deep → dreaming, 4–6 clean cycles a night.
- **20–28** — Alarm drags the sleeper out of deep sleep mid-cycle: grogginess, the grey crack.
- **28–40** — The three thieves: alcohol, late caffeine, restless fragmented night.
- **40–52** — The fix: same wake time daily; dark, cool, quiet room; let cycles finish. Colour returns.
- **52–60** — "Sleep isn't a bank. It's a rhythm." Morning light. Disclaimer on screen.

## 6. Narration script (emotion-directed am_michael, ~135 words ≈ 58s)

> *[curious, conspiratorial]* "You slept eight hours last night. So why do you feel like you got hit by a truck?
> *[shifting to teacher mode]* The answer isn't hours — it's architecture. Your brain sleeps in ninety-minute cycles: light sleep, deep sleep, then dreaming. A full night is four to six clean cycles.
> *[darker]* But if your alarm drags you out of deep sleep mid-cycle, you wake up groggy no matter how long you slept. Alcohol, late caffeine, and a restless room shatter those cycles into fragments — eight hours of broken sleep is not eight hours of sleep.
> *[warm, resolved]* The fix researchers keep coming back to: wake up at the same time every day, keep the room dark, cool, and quiet — and let the cycles finish. Sleep isn't a bank. It's a rhythm."

On-screen disclaimer (crisp DOM text, final 4s): "Educational content only — not medical advice. Talk to your doctor about persistent fatigue."

## 9. Subtitle and title design

- **Titles** laid in with the knife (stroke-by-stroke `appear` sweep ~1s), taken off by paint-over. Serif display face (Cormorant, OFL).
- **Subtitles:** plain serif, ≤ 2 lines, in a calm plane; hold ≥ max(1.8s, speech + 0.6s). Burned in + exported `.srt`.
- **Small text** (disclaimer, data labels): crisp DOM text over the painting — knife strokes can't carry small letters.
- **Loudness:** −14 LUFS; music/ambience duck ~8 dB under voice.
