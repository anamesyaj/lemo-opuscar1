# Scientific & Medical Facts: What If You Swallowed a Single Drop of Lava?

Every numerical and physiological claim in `what-if-drop-of-lava` is grounded in volcanology, thermodynamics, and clinical thermal-injury medicine:

1. **Eruption temperature and viscosity of basaltic lava (1,100–1,250 °C / ~1,200 °C; 10,000–100,000× viscosity of water)**
   - **Claim in film**: Fresh lava erupts around 1,200 °C — 12× hotter than boiling water (100 °C) — and is viscous like thick glowing honey rather than liquid water.
   - **Source**: U.S. Geological Survey (USGS), *Volcano Hazards Program — Lava Flows & Temperatures*: basaltic lava (such as Kīlauea) erupts at 1,100–1,250 °C; dynamic viscosity of basaltic magma is $10^1\text{–}10^3\text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$ ($10^4\text{–}10^5\times$ water at $10^{-3}\text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$).
   - **URL**: https://www.usgs.gov/programs/VHP

2. **Flash vaporization of saliva & the Leidenfrost vapor cushion (1,700× volume expansion)**
   - **Claim in film**: When a 1,200 °C surface contacts wet oral mucosa (saliva is ~99% water), surface moisture flashes into steam, expanding ~1,600–1,700× in volume and briefly forming an insulating Leidenfrost vapor film while still radiating intense heat that chars superficial papillae/nerve endings into a pale white third-degree burn track.
   - **Measurement / Physics**: Liquid water at 100 °C ($\rho \approx 958\text{ kg/m}^3$) vaporizing to steam at 1 atm ($\rho \approx 0.598\text{ kg/m}^3$) expands by $958 / 0.598 \approx 1,602\times$ (and further to $>1,700\times$ as steam superheats above 150 °C). Leidenfrost point of water on smooth hot silicate/oxide surfaces occurs above ~200–300 °C.
   - **Reference**: NIST Chemistry WebBook (Thermophysical Properties of Fluid Systems — Water/Steam); Walker, J. (2010), *Boiling and the Leidenfrost Effect*, Cleveland State University / *Scientific American*.

3. **Superheated steam inhalation & acute upper-airway edema (laryngeal obstruction)**
   - **Claim in film**: Superheated steam in the oral/pharyngeal cavity causes rapid mucosal swelling (thermal supraglottic/laryngeal edema) that can narrow or close the airway within seconds to minutes — the primary immediate life threat in oral/inhalation thermal burns.
   - **Source**: American Burn Association (*Advanced Burn Life Support*) & NCBI StatPearls, *Inhalation Injury*: steam carries ~4,000× the heat-transfer capacity of dry air of equal volume and causes acute upper-airway edema above the vocal cords.
   - **URL**: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK513261/

4. **Esophageal adhesion & full-thickness thermal perforation**
   - **Claim in film**: Cooling viscous molten silicate adheres to esophageal mucosa during swallowing, causing deep focal full-thickness coagulation necrosis and potential esophageal perforation (mediastinitis risk).
   - **Source**: Chirica, M. et al., *Esophageal perforation*, *Journal of Visceral Surgery* / clinical literature on ingested molten material & severe thermal esophageal burns.

5. **Thermodynamic heat budget of a single pea-sized drop (~1,000 Joules) & quenching into volcanic glass (obsidian / tachylite)**
   - **Claim in film**: A single pea-sized drop of lava (~0.2 mL, ~0.55 g at $\rho \approx 2.75\text{ g/cm}^3$) cooling from 1,200 °C to 37 °C releases roughly 1,000 Joules of total thermal energy — enough to vaporize less than half a gram of water (~0.44 g). Rather than melting through the stomach and abdomen like Hollywood acid, gastric fluid quenches the molten silicate in seconds into brittle, fractured volcanic glass (sideromelane / obsidian) under thermal shock.
   - **Calculation**:
     - Mass $m \approx 0.20\text{ cm}^3 \times 2.75\text{ g/cm}^3 = 0.55\text{ g}$.
     - Specific heat of basaltic melt/glass $c_p \approx 1.3\text{ J/(g}\cdot\text{K)}$; cooling $\Delta T = 1200 - 37 = 1163\text{ K}$:
       $Q_{\text{sensible}} = 0.55 \times 1.3 \times 1163 \approx 832\text{ J}$ (rapid glass transition suppresses most crystallization latent heat; even partial latent heat brings $Q_{\text{total}} \approx 950\text{–}1,050\text{ J} \approx 1,000\text{ J}$).
     - Latent heat of vaporization of water at 100 °C ($\Delta H_{\text{vap}} = 2,260\text{ J/g}$, plus $263\text{ J/g}$ to heat from 37 °C to 100 °C $= 2,523\text{ J/g}$):
       $m_{\text{steam}} = 1000 / 2523 \approx 0.40\text{ g}$ ($< 0.5\text{ g}$ of water).
   - **Quenching to volcanic glass**: USGS Volcano Hazards Program — *Volcanic Glass*: when magma or lava is rapidly quenched by water before mineral crystals can nucleate, it solidifies into amorphous volcanic glass (sideromelane/tachylite/obsidian) and fractures from thermal contraction stress (hyaloclastite fragmentation).
