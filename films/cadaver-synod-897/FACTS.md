# Historical Facts — The Cadaver Synod (Rome, January 897 AD)

## Primary & Secondary Sources
- **Liutprand of Cremona**, *Antapodosis* (Book I, ch. 30, c. 958 AD)
- **Auxilius of Naples**, *In defensionem sacrae ordinationis papae Formosi* (early 10th century)
- **Annales Alamannici** & **Flodoard of Reims**, *De Christi triumphis apud Italiam*
- **Horace Kinder Mann**, *The Lives of the Popes in the Early Middle Ages*, Vol. IV (1910)

## Verified Historical Details
1. **Date & Location**: January 897 AD, Basilica of St. John Lateran (*San Giovanni in Laterano*), Rome.
2. **Prosecutor**: **Pope Stephen VI** (sometimes numbered Stephen VII), backed by Lambert of Spoleto and his mother Ageltrude.
3. **Defendant**: **Pope Formosus** (reigned 891–896 AD), who had died nine months earlier in April 896 AD.
4. **The Exhumation & Courtroom Setup**:
   - Stephen VI ordered Formosus's rotting corpse exhumed from his papal tomb.
   - The skeleton/corpse was dressed in full richly embroidered papal vestments, crowned with the papal tiara/mitre, and seated upright on a papal throne in the Lateran Basilica.
5. **The Deacon Defense Lawyer**:
   - Because canon law required the accused to have counsel, a terrified young **deacon** was appointed to stand behind/beside the throne and answer on the dead Pope's behalf while Pope Stephen VI screamed accusations of perjury, violating canon law against translating sees (moving from Bishop of Porto to Bishop of Rome), and ambition.
   - An earthquake shook the Lateran Basilica around this turbulent period, which contemporaries saw as a sign of outrage.
6. **The Verdict & Aftermath**:
   - Unsurprisingly, the corpse was found guilty.
   - All of Formosus's papal acts and ordinations were declared null and void (*ACTA NULLA*).
   - The corpse was stripped of its sacred vestments, the **three fingers of his right hand** used for papal blessings were hacked off, and the body was eventually dragged through Rome and thrown into the **Tiber River**.
   - A horrified fisherman (or monk) later recovered the body from the Tiber bank, and after a public uprising deposed and strangled Stephen VI later in 897, **Pope Theodore II** and **Pope John IX** reburied Formosus in St. Peter's Basilica in full papal honors and banned any future trials of dead people forever.
