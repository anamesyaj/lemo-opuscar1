# Historical Fact Sheet: *The French Town That Sued Tiny Bugs (St-Julien, 1587)*

Every historical claim in `films/weevil-trial-1587` is verified against documented scholarship and primary archival transcriptions below. (St-Julien in Maurienne was then part of the Duchy of Savoy, now Savoie in southeastern France — referred to in modern viral retellings and historical summaries as a French commune.)

## 1. Place, Date, and Defendants
- **Location**: Parish / Commune of **Saint-Julien** (Maurienne, Duchy of Savoy; today Saint-Julien-Mont-Denis, Savoie, France).
- **Date**: Proceedings reopened **April 13, 1587** (following an earlier 1545–1546 complaint against the same pests) and continued through **June 29, 1587** (public parish square assembly at *Parloir d'Amont*) and **September 3, 1587** (legal objections by insect counsel).
- **Accused**: Green vineyard weevils (*Rhynchites auratus* / garden or vine weevils), recorded in the Latin proceedings as *"animalia bruta ad formam muscarum volantia coloris viridis communi voce appellata verpillions seu amblevins"*.
- **Sources**:
  - E. P. Evans, *The Criminal Prosecution and Capital Punishment of Animals* (London / New York: Dutton, 1906), pp. 37–50 & Appendix A: `"De actis scindicorum communitatis Sancti Julliani..."`, Project Gutenberg eBook #43286: https://www.gutenberg.org/files/43286/43286-h/43286-h.htm
  - Brill (2025), *Living according to Nature*, Chapter 2: `"On the Relation between Humans and Animals in the Context of Animal Trials"` (Section 2.2: *"Trial of Insects from St. Julien, 1587"*): https://brill.com/display/book/9789004713314/BP000003.xml

## 2. Ecclesiastical Court & Court-Appointed Defense Counsel
- **Forum**: The ecclesiastical court of the **Bishop of Maurienne** (Church courts held jurisdiction over wild swarms of insects because human authorities argued only spiritual censures/anathema could reach countless uncatchable pests, whereas secular courts tried individual domesticated animals).
- **Defense Counsel**: Canon procedure required formal legal representation for the accused insects. In 1587, **Antoine Filliol** was appointed procurator and **Pierre Rembaud** advocate for the weevils; the townsfolk were represented by syndics **François Amenet** and **Pétremand Bertrand**. *(Note: Some secondary blog summaries mix the roles of procurator and advocate or conflate names with the 1545 counsel Pierre Falcon / Claude Morel; our short narration refers accurately to "court-appointed lawyers" and depicts Filliol arguing canon law.)*
- **Defense Argument**: Counsel argued that God created animals before humanity, blessed them, and granted them a prior right under natural and Divine law to feed upon green herbs and vegetation (*"animas vegetativas"*); therefore the weevils could not be summarily excommunicated merely for eating in the vineyards.
- **Quotes**:
  - Evans (1906), p. 42–45 & Brill (2025): *"Two suitable advocates were assigned to the insects, who argued on their behalf that these creatures were created before man, and had been blessed by God, who gave them the right to feed on grass..."* (Martinengo-Cesaresco, 1909, p. 350, citing Evans).

## 3. Proposed Settlement: *Le Grand Feisse* ("Wildlife Reserve")
- **Offer (June 29, 1587)**: Seeing their prosecution stall, St-Julien's townspeople voted in public assembly (*Parloir d'Amont*) to offer the insects a dedicated plot of land outside the vineyards called **Le Grand Feisse** for their perpetual subsistence, provided they abandoned the vines or accepted excommunication if they returned (retaining only human transit & mineral rights).
- **Rejection (September 3, 1587)**: Weevil procurator Antoine Filliol formally inspected *Le Grand Feisse* and rejected the offer in court, declaring the designated terrain was **sterile and far too barren/small** to support the insect population; plaintiff counsel insisted it contained trees and shrubs, prompting the court to appoint expert surveyors to evaluate the plot.
- **Quote**:
  - Brill (2025), Section 2.2: *"They proposed to designate certain areas for the maintenance of these insects, which they called Le Grand Feisse... The last surviving information from this case is that the defense rejected the given settlement proposal, arguing that the designated areas were too small to support the population of weevils."*

## 4. Lost Verdict Ending (Destroyed by Insects / Pests)
- **Outcome**: The final judicial decision and land-survey verdict are lost to history because the concluding pages of the 29-document dossier abruptly terminate where pages were chewed away by pests / insects / rodents in the archive.
- **Quote**:
  - Brill (2025), Section 2.2: *"The outcome of this case is unknown, as the verdict was destroyed by bookworms."*
  - EsoterX (2016) / Evans (1906) summary: *"the last two pages of the court records ... were suspiciously eaten by bugs."*
